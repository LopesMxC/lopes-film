# Research for a film

Search and read the web with your own tools (web search, fetch); when the person has the browser beside the chat
open on a page, that page is what they mean by "this". Do the research once, keep it small.

- **Read before you write**: fetch the pages the person gave, then search for what is missing. Prefer the original
  source (the company, the paper, the official page) to a summary of it.
- **Keep it in the project**: write what you found to `research/<topic>.md` as short notes: the claim, the number, the
  source URL. A film's script is written from those notes, not from memory, and the person can open them.
- **Quote little**: use facts and your own words; a quote is a few words with its source. Never copy an article.
- **Pictures and footage**: link to where they are and say what licence the page states; download or place in the
  film only what the person owns, what is public domain or CC0, or what they tell you they may use.
- **Say what you could not find** and what is uncertain, in one line each, so the film does not state it as fact.
- End with the sources as a short list of links; the person checks the work from it.
