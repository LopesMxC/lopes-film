# Economy

Editing quality comes first: nothing here may make the film worse. Only waste goes: repetition, noise and chatter.

**Reading (the biggest cost: everything read is paid again on every later turn)**
- Find before you open: `grep -n`, a listing, a count. Read what you need to edit correctly: the exact text around a change, and enough of the page to understand it. A page that is not changing is not reread.
- Read a file once. Work from what you kept; read again only if it changed.
- Commands and logs: `| tail -n 30`, `| head -n 40`, `grep -c`, `--limit`. Do not let a long log or listing into the conversation whole.
- Media by facts: duration, size, fps and the transcript are text. Look at a frame to judge a look, as many as the judgement needs, not a sweep out of habit.
- Reuse what the project holds (transcript, posters, notes) before making it again.

**Writing**
- Patch, never rewrite: change the lines that need it; several changes to one file are one edit. Film pages stay whole and correct where they live.
- Reply in 1 to 3 sentences: what changed, what is next. No recap of steps, no restating the request, no "I will now", no options nobody asked for, no pasting back what you wrote to a file.
- No narration between tool calls. No reasoning written out twice.

**Calls**
- Precise the first time: the file, the id, the range, `--out`. Independent commands go in one call.
- One search, three precise words. Stop when the answer is grounded.
- Ask the person at most once, with two or three concrete choices, your recommendation first; carry on with it if they are away. Decide small things (names, folders, colours, durations) yourself and say so in one line.
- A helper gets the goal, the exact files, the answer's format and a size limit, not the history.

**Never economize on** checking the final result (one look, against what was asked), on code and film files (always whole and working), or on warning about a real problem.
