# Explainers

An explainer takes one person from "I don't get it" to "I could say it myself". It teaches one thing.

## Plan
1. **The question** the video answers, in one sentence the viewer would ask.
2. **The answer** in one sentence. The video proves and shows it.
3. **The steps**: three to five, each one understandable only if the one before was. Cut anything the answer does not need.
4. **The example**: one concrete case that returns in each step.

## Script
- Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea each, the word the viewer uses (not the field's). Define a term the first time, with a picture.
- Pace: 140–160 words per minute for narration, with a half-second pause between ideas. A 90 s explainer is about 200 words.
- Open with the problem or question, never with "In this video". Close with the answer said again in new words and one next step.
- Read it aloud. Wherever you stumble, rewrite.

## Scenes
- One scene per step, 8–20 s each. Within a scene, change the picture on every new idea (3–6 s).
- Show before you tell: the picture appears on, or just before, the word that names it, never after.
- Use a visual metaphor only if it is exact (a pipe for a flow, a stack for a stack). A decorative metaphor is noise.
- Animate to explain: draw a line to show the order, move a part to show the change, highlight what the voice is saying and dim the rest. Everything that moves means something. See the `motion` skill.
- On-screen text: key terms and numbers only, 2–6 words, never the whole narration. A diagram carries labels the voice does not say.
- Keep one visual system (2 fonts, 3–4 colours, one illustration style) and the same object always the same colour.

## Sound
Clear narration first, music 18–22 dB under it, low and unobtrusive, a soft sound where a new step begins.

## Check
Mute the voice: do the pictures still tell the order? Remove the pictures: does the script still make sense? Then `lopesfilm look` at the start of every scene for overlap, tiny text and anything unreadable at phone size.
