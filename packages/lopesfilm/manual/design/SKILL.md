# Design for the screen

- **Hierarchy**: one thing is the most important on each frame. Size, weight and contrast say what, not decoration. Two type sizes per frame is usually enough; three at most.
- **Type**: one family, two weights. Titles tight (line-height 1.0 to 1.15), body looser (1.3 to 1.5). Never below 5% of the frame height for text a viewer must read, 8% for vertical video.
- **Contrast**: text against its background at least 4.5:1. Over footage use a soft shadow, a gradient under the text or a solid plate, not a thin outline.
- **Colour**: the project's palette first. One accent colour for emphasis, neutrals for the rest. Do not put saturated red on saturated blue.
- **Spacing**: use a grid and keep the same margins on every page. Safe area: keep 5% inside every edge, 12% at the bottom of vertical video where platforms draw their own interface.
- **Alignment**: align to something. Centre-align short titles only; left-align anything longer than one line.
- **Motion**: move with purpose. Ease in and out (never linear); 200 to 450 ms for entrances, shorter for exits; stagger a group by 60 to 100 ms. Do not animate more than two things at once. Let the final state hold long enough to be read (about 0.3 s per word).
- **Consistency**: lower thirds, captions and titles of one film share fonts, colours, corner radius and motion.
- **Check**: look at the frame at the size a phone shows it. If you squint and the main thing is not obvious, simplify.
