# Cutting and pacing

Watch (or read the transcript of) all the footage first. Decide what the film says in one sentence, then cut everything that does not serve it.

- **Rhythm**: talking head shots hold 3 to 6 seconds before something changes (a cut, a punch-in, b-roll, a caption). Never leave the same framing on screen for more than about 8 seconds unless the moment earns it. Let a strong line breathe: no cut for half a second after it.
- **Where to cut**: on an action or a breath, not in the middle of a word. Remove silences longer than about 0.4 s, filler words ("uh", "hum", "né", "tipo") and false starts. Keep a short pause before a punchline.
- **J and L cuts**: let the sound of the next shot start slightly before its picture (J), or the sound of the last one run over the next picture (L) by 4 to 8 frames. Hard simultaneous cuts feel mechanical.
- **B-roll**: cover jump cuts and abstract statements with a relevant shot of 1.5 to 4 seconds. Ken Burns (slow zoom) on still images, never a static still for more than 3 seconds. Never b-roll over a line the speaker lands with emotion.
- **Zoom punch-ins**: alternate 100% and about 110 to 120% framing on consecutive cuts of a single talking shot, to hide jump cuts. Do it on the cut, not mid-shot.
- **Sound**: dialogue first and clean. Music sits 15 to 20 dB under speech and ducks when someone talks; cut music on a beat. Add a short sound effect only where something appears or moves.
- **Captions**: 1 to 3 words per beat for fast short-form, a short line for calm ones, always inside the safe area, readable on any background. Emphasize the key word with colour or scale, not with a different font.
- **Pacing by format**: vertical short-form moves every 1.5 to 3 seconds; explainers every 4 to 7; interviews and calm films longer. Match the person's brief.
- **Check**: after cutting, look at the first 3 seconds (the hook), every transition, and the last shot. Fix what you would skip on a second viewing.
